# Stock market basics

The **stock market** is a place where you can buy small ownership pieces of companies. In Nepal, this happens on **NEPSE** (Nepal Stock Exchange).

## What is a share?

A share is a tiny slice of a company. If a company has 1,000,000 shares total and you own 1,000 of them, you own **0.1%** of the company.

## What is NEPSE?

NEPSE is Nepal's only stock exchange. It opens **Sunday to Thursday, 11 AM to 3 PM**. Outside these hours, prices don't change.

## How do you make money?

Two ways:

1. **Price goes up** — you bought at Rs 200, now it's Rs 300, you can sell for Rs 100 profit per share.
2. **Dividend** — the company gives a portion of its profit to shareholders, usually once a year.

## What are circuit breakers?

NEPSE limits how much a stock's price can move in a single day — usually **±10%**. This stops panic crashes and prevents wild manipulation. If a stock "hits the upper circuit", buyers want it but nobody will sell at that price.

## Sectors

NEPSE groups companies into sectors:

- **Commercial Banks** (e.g. NABIL, SBI)
- **Hydropower** (e.g. CHCL, UPPER)
- **Insurance**
- **Microfinance**
- **Hotel & Tourism**
- **Manufacturing**
- **Investment**

Different sectors have different normal P/E ratios. Comparing a bank's valuation to a hydropower company's is not always fair.

## Key terms to remember

- **LTP**: Last Traded Price — the most recent transaction price.
- **Volume / Qty**: how many shares traded in a session.
- **52-week high/low**: highest and lowest price in the last year.
- **EPS**: Earnings Per Share — company profit divided by total shares.
- **P/E ratio**: Price ÷ EPS — how many years of earnings you're paying for.
