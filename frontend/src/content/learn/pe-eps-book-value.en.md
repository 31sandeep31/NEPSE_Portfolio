# P/E, EPS, Book value — simple explanations

These three numbers tell you whether a stock is "expensive" or "cheap" relative to the company's actual business.

## EPS — Earnings Per Share

EPS is the company's yearly profit divided by total shares.

> If a company makes Rs 1 billion profit and has 100 million shares, EPS = Rs 10.

**Higher EPS = more profit per share = generally better.** But always compare with the same company's previous EPS — is it growing or shrinking?

## P/E ratio — Price to Earnings

P/E = current share price ÷ EPS.

It tells you: **how many years of current earnings does the share price cost?**

> Share price Rs 200, EPS Rs 10 → P/E = 20. You're paying 20 years of current earnings.

**What's a good P/E?** It depends on the sector:

| Sector | Typical P/E |
|---|---|
| Commercial Banks | 10 – 18 |
| Hydropower | 15 – 30 |
| Microfinance | 12 – 22 |
| Hotel & Tourism | varies widely |

A bank with P/E 35 is unusually expensive. A hydropower with P/E 35 might be normal for that sector.

**P/E is not magic.** A high P/E can mean:
- The market expects future growth (could be right or wrong).
- The stock is overhyped (will likely come back down).
- Earnings are temporarily low and price hasn't adjusted.

## Book Value

Book value is the per-share "accounting net worth" of the company.

> If the company has Rs 100 of assets and Rs 50 of debt, the equity is Rs 50. Divide by total shares to get per-share book value.

**P/B ratio** = price ÷ book value. P/B of 1.0 means you're paying exactly the accounting value. P/B above 1 means the market values the business more than its books — common for healthy companies. P/B below 1 can be a value opportunity, or a warning that the business is in trouble.

## How this app uses these numbers

In the per-stock detail page, you'll see P/E, EPS, and Book value displayed clearly. In the signals system:

- If your stock's P/E is much higher than its sector median, we flag **Valuation stretched**.
- If yield (dividend ÷ price) is high enough (above 8%), we flag **Strong dividend — usually a hold**.

These are heuristics, not guarantees. They flag situations worth thinking about.
